What this is, and who pays

The city already publishes every violation. Propwatch reads all of it for you, tells you what it means, and warns you before a fine grows.

The data is free and scattered across five city systems. The product is putting it in one place and watching it for you.

How it works

1 · ADDRESSSomeone types an address. NYC Planning's free lookup turns it into the property's tax-lot and building IDs.
2 · RECORDSSix public datasets are asked about that ID: housing, buildings, penalties, complaints, 311.
3 · SUMMARYOne plain-English page: what's open, how hazardous, what's owed. Every month it checks again and tells you what changed.

Who would pay

Best first bet

Owners, co-op boards, property managers

Pain: violations and filing deadlines get missed, and fines grow. Offer: monthly watch on your buildings with a plain-English "what's new and what to do".

Guess to test: roughly $20–50 a month per building or portfolio.

Second

Buyers, brokers, lenders, insurers

Pain: surprises found after closing. Offer: a records report on any address before a deal.

Guess to test: a per-report fee. Crowded; others sell property reports.

Mission

Tenant groups, journalists, council offices

Pain: neglect is hard to see across a neighborhood. Offer: the neighborhood view, free. Builds trust and attention.

Little budget, so grants or sponsorship, not sales.

Not proven yet. No one has said they'd pay. Other free tools already show this data to tenants. Before building more, ask 5 small landlords or co-op board members: how do you find out about violations today, what has it cost you, and would you pay to hear sooner?

Propwatch reports what is on record. It never says a building is safe, and it is not a substitute for a licensed inspector.
